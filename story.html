The White Paper

It started with a simple questionWhat is my legacy?

I was scrolling through social media one evening when I landed on a channel that stopped people driving exceptional cars and asked them for their keys to success. The advice was so good I went back, watched all eight of them again, and took notes.

Afterward something nagged at me. Every one of those people had mastered the economy. Their children and grandchildren were probably set for life. But none of them had changed the rules that make getting ahead so hard for everyone else. They had beaten the game, not fixed it.

Built, lost, rebuilt

Business came naturally to me early. Then the 2008 crash took down my real estate development and mortgage banking companies. The years that followed were a grind: a corporate mortgage job, an internet lending venture for a private equity firm that ended after eighteen months, and finally a new mortgage and real estate brokerage built with people I trust.

We kept the house, put two great kids through out-of-state colleges, and kept going. I learned to run my personal life like a business, to work the late nights and weekends, and eventually to like the grind. It made me humble and thankful. A health scare along the way reminded me what actually matters: faith, family, health, relationships. Money is the soil you work so those things can grow.

The question

Thinking about a second act, I pictured the usual: a lake house with a boat, a coastal cottage, a cabin in the middle of nowhere. Then I asked what the point would be. I wanted something with more meaning — a legacy for my kids and future grandkids that also made a difference for people outside my own family.

Leaving your kids better off helps your own clan. It does nothing about why it keeps getting harder for everyone to get ahead.

That question led me to taxes, and then to the one tax I handle every single day in my work. That's the idea.
