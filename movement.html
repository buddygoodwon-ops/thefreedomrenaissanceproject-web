Eliminating the property tax in one county is the goal. But it isn't the end. It's the spark. Once people see it done somewhere real, it stops being a crazy idea and starts being a choice. That's the Movement.

1. The First Follower

In his famous 2010 TED talk, "How to Start a Movement," Derek Sivers plays a short clip of a hillside concert. One shirtless guy starts dancing alone. He looks ridiculous. Then one person joins him — and everything changes. The first follower turns a lone nut into a leader. A second and third follower make it a group. Soon newcomers join because joining feels safer than standing out, and within minutes the whole hillside is dancing.

Sivers' point: leadership is overrated. The first follower is what actually starts a movement, because they show everyone else how to follow.

The idea is the dancer. Registering is how you become the first follower.

2. The Hundredth Monkey

In the 1950s, researchers on Japan's Kōshima island watched a young macaque begin washing sweet potatoes in the sea. Others copied her, one by one, until the habit spread through the troop. Writers later turned the story into a parable: once enough individuals adopt a new behavior — a "hundredth monkey" — it suddenly becomes normal for everyone.

Scientists have pointed out that the dramatic version is a legend; the real spread was gradual and happened through imitation. But that's exactly our plan. People learn by watching their neighbors. The parable is honest about one thing: there is a tipping point, and after it, change is no longer strange.

3. An Idea Whose Time Has Come

Victor Hugo wrote that no army can resist an idea whose time has come. Look around: states have dropped their income taxes and grown fastest. Permitless carry went from a handful of states to more than half. Florida voters just weighed a constitutional amendment aimed at ending homestead property taxes. North Dakota put abolition on the ballot. The question is no longer whether people want this. It's where it will be proven first.
